Ground within 1.5 km is drawn smooth from the 1 m height model, textured by land type from the map; beyond that it is coarser and fades into haze. Rock on steep slopes and detail finer than a metre are drawn, not measured. Roof shapes are fitted to the 1 m surface model; where none fits, the roof is drawn flat and plain. Wall cladding is drawn; on other buildings the eaves, roof tiles and straightened outlines are drawn too. The listing house is drawn only as measured: its walls stand at the measured roof edge and its roof is plain. It is highlighted, and its colours are not its real colours. No windows or doors are shown on any building, because where they are is not known. Whether a tree looks like a conifer or a broadleaf follows its measured shape, not its species. Shade from the ground is worked out from this world's height data for the chosen date and hour; the shadows of trees and buildings come from their drawn shapes. The boundary line is approximate.

Sun and shade

    How the shade is drawn

    The sun's place is worked out in the page for the chosen date and time, and the time is the site's own clock, with its zone. The date runs over one year, the year the measured figures are for.

    Shade from the ground is worked out from this world's height data: from the 1 m heights near you, then the 5 m and 20 m heights out to the edge of the world. Mountains further away than that can only stop the sun, never draw a shadow: where the measured horizon says they hide the sun, the whole view is shown in their shade.

    The shadows of trees and buildings come from their drawn shapes, near you only. They are drawn, not measured.

    "Measured" lines come from the measured figures (see Specs): clear sky, the terrain only, at eye height at the garden point. "Drawn" lines speak only about this world's heights.
